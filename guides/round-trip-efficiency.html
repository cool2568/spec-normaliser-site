Round-trip efficiency: what it means and why datasheets differ

Round-trip efficiency is the share of the energy put into a battery that comes back out, and its value depends on where the measurement starts and ends and on how the test is run. Two datasheets' percentages are comparable only when both describe the same boundary under similar conditions.

Definition

Round-trip efficiency (RTE) is the energy discharged divided by the energy charged, over a cycle that starts and ends at the same state of charge. Charge 10 kWh, get 9 kWh back, and the RTE is 90%. The missing energy becomes heat: in the cells' internal resistance, where the loss grows with the square of the current; in the battery management electronics; in any power conversion; and in auxiliary loads such as fans, heaters and standby consumption.

DC and AC round-trip efficiency

A DC figure is measured at the battery's terminals and covers the cells and battery management. An AC figure is measured at the AC connection, so it also includes the inverter twice: AC to DC to charge, and DC to AC to discharge. For the same battery the AC figure is therefore lower; it is roughly the DC figure times the efficiency of each conversion.

Enphase's IQ Battery 5P sheet shows both: 90% AC round-trip efficiency, measured "AC to the battery to AC at 50% power rating at 25°C", and 96% DC round-trip efficiency for the battery module. The gap implies the two conversions together pass about 94% of the energy (0.90 ÷ 0.96). Our comparison pages show the 90%, the first round-trip figure on that sheet.

BYD's Battery-Box Premium HVS 5.1 sheet gives a round-trip efficiency of at least 96% without saying AC or DC. The Battery-Box connects to a separate inverter, so read the figure as battery-side unless BYD says otherwise, and don't set it against Enphase's 90% AC figure.

Tesla's Powerwall 3 sheet has no round-trip row, so its comparison pages show a dash. It gives a "Solar to Battery to Home/Grid Efficiency" of 89%, at 25 °C, at beginning of life and at 3.3 kW charge and discharge power, and a "Solar to Home/Grid Efficiency" of 97.5% by the CEC weighted method. Both start at the DC solar input and end at the AC output: a third boundary, comparable with neither of the others as it stands.

Test conditions that move the number

  • Power. Resistive loss grows with the square of the current, so a slow cycle loses less in the cells; at very low power, fixed losses such as standby consumption weigh more.
  • Temperature. Internal resistance rises in the cold. Enphase and Tesla both state 25 °C.
  • State-of-charge window. A partial cycle, say from 10% to 90%, is not comparable with a full one.
  • Age. Internal resistance rises as a battery ages, so efficiency falls; Tesla states its figure at beginning of life.
  • Auxiliary loads. Ask whether cooling, heating and standby power are inside the figure.

How to compare fairly

  1. Find each figure's boundary: DC at the battery, AC to AC through the battery's own inverter, or a mixed path such as solar to battery to home.
  2. Put both on one boundary. For a DC battery, combine its DC figure with the charge and discharge efficiencies of the inverter it will be paired with; for AC units, compare AC with AC.
  3. Match the conditions: power, temperature, state-of-charge window and age.
  4. Ask for the test method. IEC 62933-2-1 sets out unit parameters and test methods for electrical energy storage systems, round-trip efficiency among them.
  5. Convert to energy: each cycle loses the charged energy × (1 − RTE). Charging 10 kWh a day, 90% loses 1 kWh a day and 96% loses 0.4 kWh.

FAQ

Why is AC round-trip efficiency lower than DC?

An AC figure includes the inverter's conversions in both directions, AC to DC when charging and DC to AC when discharging, on top of the battery's own losses. Enphase's IQ Battery 5P sheet gives 90% AC and 96% DC for the same product.

Does round-trip efficiency change as a battery ages?

Yes. Internal resistance rises with age, so more energy turns into heat on each cycle. A datasheet figure describes a new battery unless it says otherwise; Tesla's Powerwall 3 sheet says so explicitly.

Is round-trip efficiency the same as inverter efficiency?

No. Inverter efficiency covers one conversion in one direction. Round-trip efficiency covers a full charge and discharge, through the battery and, for an AC figure, through the inverter twice.
